1. Big errors
1. g₂ has two definitions that differ by a factor of about 2.
* Appendix B, L1019, defines g₂ = E_J sin ε_p φ_a²φ_b. This matches the simulated model (static flux, ω_p = 0).
* Every figure uses g₂ = J₁(ε_p) E_J φ_a²φ_b instead. J₁(ε_p)/sin ε_p is between 0.50 and 0.53 over your grid.
* I checked: the period-doubling at ε_p = 0.627 is 41.0 MHz with J₁, as quoted in App. E, but 80.6 MHz with the paper's own definition.
* So every g₂ axis is about half the real coupling, and ε_d/2g₂ is about twice the real cat size.
* The hidden \iffalse block (L1349-1353) already notes this factor, but it does not appear in the PDF.
* The main text also never links g, g₂, ε_p and E_J, yet ε_p appears as an axis in Fig. 16.
2. κ_b has two definitions that differ by a factor of 2.
* Eqs. (5)–(6) use the master-equation (GKSL) convention, κ_b 𝒟[b], which gives ḃ = −(κ_b/2) b.
* App. B (L939) uses ḃ = −κ_b b. The Julia term −2κ_b ẋ_b matches App. B, not Eq. (6).
* So "κ_b/2π = 48 MHz" in the classical figures is a photon-loss rate of 96 MHz in the convention of Eq. (5).
* You need to check which convention the quantum OTOC and IPR runs used. If they differ, the conclusion that the quantum threshold is lower is partly an artefact.
3. Fig. 4 has the wrong periods and the wrong quantity.
* The satellites have period 2π/ω_a = 4π/ω_b (App. B, L1052). The captions should read T = 4π/ω_b and 8π/ω_b, not 2π/ω_b and 4π/ω_b.
* Fig. 4e plots the real part of the multipliers, but you claim a Neimark–Sacker bifurcation (a complex pair leaving the unit circle). That requires |μ|, and |μ| = 1 is crossed before Re μ = 1.
* The bullet list at L380-384 leaves out the +1 crossing (how satellites are born) and the complex-pair crossing (the one you actually observe).
* Footnote 10 is wrong: one complex pair plus two real multipliers is also possible.
4. The chaos is transient, and the Lyapunov maps depend on the integration window.
* App. I (Fig. 18) shows the chaotic fraction going to 0 by κ_b t ≈ 200.
* The Lyapunov window is 45/κ_b, about the same as the transient lifetime you quote (42/κ_b).
* So the red region in Fig. 5d means "still chaotic at the end of the window". Your headline threshold depends on the window length.
* The method itself (T, T_tr, d₀) is inside \iffalse (L1310-1381), so the PDF has no Lyapunov method at all.
5. The headline threshold is about the vacuum start, not the cat.
* Starting at the origin "because of the fringes" (L443) does not hold up: a classical trajectory does not see quantum interference fringes.
* A better reason: the origin is the saddle between the two cat basins. It is where the bit-flip path goes and where a cat is prepared from vacuum.
* Fig. 14 shows the cat attractor stays regular almost everywhere. Your hidden text says it outright: "chaos is a property of the initial condition, not an instability of the cat."
* So the abstract's "upper threshold on g₂ and cat size" is overstated. This needs to be in the main text, and the abstract rephrased.
6. The main text does not show the link between the satellite instability and chaos.
* In Fig. 5d, the Hill line sits 10–20 MHz to the left of the chaos boundary. A reader sees disagreement.
* The agreement only appears in Fig. 15 (satellite start), which is in Appendix E. Move it to the main text.
7. The physics in the model section is wrong in places.
* L315: V ∝ sin(φ/Φ₀) is wrong. The Josephson relations are I = I_c sin φ and V = φ₀ φ̇.
* L329: no choice of the offset φ_d turns sin(x + φ_d) into sin x − x. You need the real mechanism for removing the linear term.
* App. H assumes a pumped flux ε(t) = ε_p sin(ω_p t). The main model is autoparametric with a static flux, so the two are not consistent.
8. The quantum truncation is probably too small.
* IPR uses n_a = 29. But the satellites sit at |a| ≈ 6, about 36 photons (Fig. 12), and the chaotic region reaches |a| ≈ 7.5 (Fig. 5c).
* So N_eff ≈ 9.7 may be limited by the truncation. The OTOC truncation is not given at all.
* The IPR time T = 0.2/κ_b is 0.66 ns, about 2.7 memory periods. That is shorter than 1/g₂, so the IPR drop may just be the bare cat relaxing to its dressed state, not chaos.
9. Fig. 11 contradicts Appendix H.
* The figure labels one ATS "Buffer" and the other "Memory", and couples the port to both.
* App. H says the buffer and memory are the symmetric and antisymmetric modes of the two ATS, and that the memory is dark to the port.
* Also, without L_c, ω_b = 2ω_a needs C_c = 1.5 C̄. You should say whether that is realistic.
10. The mitigation comparisons are made at fixed g₂.
* What matters for the qubit is κ₂ = 4g₂²/κ_b at a fixed α².
* Larger κ_b: at your working point the threshold in Fig. 7 is flat. So raising κ_b lowers κ₂ without moving the threshold, which makes things worse at first.
* Multi-buffer: with N identical buffers, the symmetric combination couples with g_eff/√N in the rotating-wave approximation. The apparent gain may come only from the choice of x-axis.
11. "Infinitely many limit cycles" (L360) is not supported. Your own equation J₂(2|a₀|) = ε′/2β′ has finitely many roots, because J₂ decays.

3. Open questions
For the main text:
1. Does the cat itself ever lose stability? Run Hill's method on the cat orbit, not only on the satellites.
2. What does chaos do to the qubit? Show bit-flip time or leakage against g₂. You already have Floquet–Arnoldi bit-flip data in analysis/slowest_decay_rate/. Without this, "chaos is bad" is never tied to a qubit metric.
3. Are the satellites a leakage risk? They are stable attractors well below the chaos threshold, which makes them leakage states. Where are they born, and how large are their basins?
4. Where are real devices on the map? Mark them, and state g₂/κ_b. At your threshold it is about 0.85–1.7, well outside the regime where the buffer can be adiabatically eliminated.
5. Is there a real chaotic attractor, or only transient chaos? Does the transient lifetime diverge at some g₂ (a crisis)? Is the route period-doubling, then Neimark–Sacker, then torus breakdown? Poincaré sections of the torus would show it.
6. Why is the quantum threshold lower? OTOCs also grow exponentially near an unstable saddle without any chaos, and the origin is such a saddle. Compare with a classical OTOC from truncated Wigner with the same initial state.
7. Are the mitigations still better at fixed κ₂ and α²?
For the appendices:
* A Hill-method appendix. Cover the convergence in the number of harmonics N_H, how the genuine eigenvalues are selected, the Liouville check, and a cross-check against a directly integrated monodromy matrix.
* The Lyapunov method. Un-hide it, add convergence in T and d₀, and define the yellow line.
* Quantum simulation details. Give the truncations and their convergence, how the sine of an operator is computed, the OTOC fit window, the initial state (bare or dressed cat), and the κ_b convention.
* Robustness checks. Test memory loss κ_a ≠ 0, the drive frequency corrected for the Stark shift (utils/solve_resonance.py exists), the drive phase, and E_J asymmetry for the parity-protected design.
* Fig. 7 slope (\TMD{Explanation?}). This is my hypothesis, not tested: the satellite existence condition 2κ′_b|y| ≤ ε′_d (Eqs. B21–B23) gives a threshold linear in κ_b. Overlay it on Fig. 7 to check.
* Symmetry. I checked that the rotating-frame equations (B3) are invariant under a → −a combined with τ → τ + π. So the full model already has a parity tied to a half-period time shift. Say what the parity-protected design adds (an instantaneous parity). Then check whether period doubling is forbidden by symmetry there or only pushed to larger g₂.
